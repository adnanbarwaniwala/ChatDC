OVERVIEW:
The Daly College, commonly referred to as DC, was founded in 1870 by Sir Henry Daly.
It is a co-ed day-cum boarding school. Boarding is offered from the 4th grade.

History:
- Founded in 1870 as the Residency School by Sir Henry Daly, the institution was established to educate the children of royalty and nobility in the Central Indian Princely States.
- In 1882, it was renamed The Daly College in honor of its founder.
- The main building, designed in the Indo-Saracenic architectural style by Col. Sir Swinton Jacob, was inaugurated on November 8, 1912, by the Viceroy, Lord Hardinge.
- Originally exclusive to the sons of Central Indian princes, the college opened its doors to all students based on merit in 1940.
- In 2005, Daly College became a member of the Round Square, an international network of schools.

Motto:
The Sanskrit motto "Gyanamev Shakti" translates to "Knowledge is Power."

Vision Statement:
- To foster a democratic environment that encourages lifelong participation in the acquisition of knowledge and is supportive, innovative, and demanding of excellence, combining the best of traditions with modernity.
- To develop well-rounded, independent-thinking, and confident individuals with leadership and communication skills who are socially, emotionally, and spiritually secure.
- To create global citizens with strong values, who are environmentally and socially conscious and who have the ability to constantly drive and benefit from change.


LOCATION:
The Daly College is located in Indore, Madhya Pradesh. Situated on the Malwa plateau in central India, Indore is well connected by rail, road and air. There is air connectivity with Mumbai, Delhi, Pune, Bangalore, Kolkata and other big cities. The climate is temperate and very pleasant, except in May when it gets warm.

EDUCATIONAL BOARDS:
The school has 2 educational boards. The first is CBSE. The students start off with this board from the first class till the fifth class and in 6th grade are offered an option to switch to another board.
This is the second board: CI (Cambridge International) Board which originates from the UK. Though this is not compulsory. The students can choose to continue with the CBSE board as well in 6th grade. The CI board has exams in the 8th, 10th, 11th and 12th standards.
The CBSE Board has exams in the 10th and 12th grade.

The Cambridge International (CI) Section at Daly College offers a structured academic pathway from Grade VI to Grade XII, providing globally recognized qualifications such as Cambridge IGCSE and Cambridge International AS and A Levels. These programs are designed to develop learners who are confident, responsible, innovative, and engaged.
Program Structure:
1. Cambridge Checkpoint (Grades VII and VIII):
   - Serves as a curriculum framework for learners aged 11-14.
   - Incorporates internationally benchmarked tests marked by Cambridge International, providing feedback on students' strengths and weaknesses.

2. International General Certificate of Secondary Education (IGCSE) (Grades IX and X):
   - A comprehensive two-year program equivalent to Grade X examinations of CBSE and ICSE in India.
   - Encourages high academic standards through a practical approach to teaching and learning.
   - Develops skills in creative thinking, enquiry, and problem-solving.

3. Cambridge International AS and A Levels (Grades XI and XII):
   - Internationally benchmarked qualifications providing excellent preparation for university education.
   - Offer a choice of 60 different subjects, allowing learners to specialize or follow a broad course of study.
   - Assessment includes a variety of methods such as written examinations, orals, practicals, projects, and coursework.

Eligibility and Admission:
- CI is offered from Grade VI.
- Eligibility criterion includes securing 70% in English in the Half Yearly exam of Grade VI.
- In case of more than 30 applicants, selection is based on merit.
- Selected students move to Grade VI (CI) in March of the following year.

Each student in the school is part of a house that has a teacher as the House In-charge.
There are two boys day-boarding houses: Tagore and Jawahar.
There are two girls day-boarding houses: Indira and Ahilya.

Inter House competitions are played between the above Houses.

However, students while belonging to the above Houses actually reside in age specific residences. For boys these are Malwa House, Holkar House and the three main Houses. For girls these are Bharati Junior and Bharati.

SPORTS:
Swimming:
The school has produced some excellent swimmers over the years. In order to give a further boost to this activity, the school has a 50-meter-long Olympic size Swimming pool.

Cricket:
Cricket Having been the home for great cricketers of the past, the school continues to produce cricketers of extreme talent and promise. Besides doing well at the IPSC and other levels, the school cricket teams also have exchanges with similar public schools in the country and overseas.

Basketball:
The school has extraordinary excellent facilities for Basketball and can boast of having produced sparkling cagers who are donming the caps of renowned universities and institutions. The courts remain a hub for Basketball activities throughout the year and many national level tournaments have been organized in the last few years.

Shooting:
The school has two shooting ranges for its students. An outdoor shooting range has existed for decades now while an Indoor one (donated by the 1979 batch) has come up this year. The students participate in the national level tourneys associated with this sport.

Football:

Squash:
The facilities for Squash have improved by leaps and bounds on the last few years. The school can now boast of six courts of International standards. Students have done well at the national level and prestigious tournaments are being organized at the DC courts.

Athletics:
The Daly College has produced eminent athletes over the decades. The Annual Athletics Meet of the school is a prestigious event. DC is also looking forward to bringing up a new athletic track.

The school also has facilities for climbing, golf, judo, badminton, tennis, skating and volleyball. It is also trying hard to regain its pristine glory in Gymnastics.


SCHOOL FACILITIES:
Temple:
The college temple was built by the Indore Durbar and funded by His Highness Charkhari. His Highness the Maharaja of Udaipur provided the "Murtis" for the temple, which was inaugurated on 28th August 1910. Built in the Malwa style, the temple houses a beautiful idol of Radha-Krishna. It features metallic bells hanging from the ceiling, a pointed peak, entrance steps, a narrow path along the edges, a Tulsi plant, and a shady Peepal tree. The temple also has tiles with painted depictions of Lord Ganesh and includes a small exquisite temple dedicated to Lord Shiva. It is open daily in the morning and evening, with services organized on festivals and special occasions.

Mosque:
The College Mosque was built by Her Highness the Begum of Bhopal and opened on 19th December 1910. Located on the northern side of the campus facing west, it features four large domes resembling minarets. Upon entering, there is an open passageway leading to a large hall adorned with wall hangings depicting holy sites like Mecca and Madina. The mosque includes small rooms for individuals to prepare for prayers (Namaz). Daily, Muslim students and teachers perform prayers here, with special congregational prayers held every Friday.

Mess:
The menu is carefully planned to ensure nutritious and balanced meals. Non-vegetarian food is prepared separately in the kitchen. There are two dining halls: one for senior students and another for juniors. The Students' Mess Committee influences menu planning. A new state-of-the-art kitchen with modern equipment is being planned to meet international standards.

Craft Technology Design Center:
This state-of-the-art hobby center caters to the creative talents of students. It offers spacious and aesthetic facilities for Fine Arts, Sculpture, Pottery, Woodcraft, Photography, Textile Designing, as well as Music, Dance, Band, and Dramatics among performing arts.

Laboratories:
The school has fully equipped laboratories for Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Biotechnology, Computer Science (C++ and Web Technology), Information Practices, Geography, Mathematics, and Language studies.

Auditorium:
An air-conditioned auditorium with a capacity of 1,200 people serves as a central venue for students to showcase their creative abilities. Various competitions and functions are organized here regularly. Daily assemblies are also held in this auditorium.

Hospital:
The Daly College Infirmary has been operational for 88 years and is currently headed by pediatrician Dr. Ankur Roy, supported by a team of four nurses. It operates 24/7, handling minor illnesses, trauma, and injuries with precision. The infirmary also has a visiting dentist, Dr. Atul Jajoo (MDS Orthodontist), and physiotherapists Dr. Jyoti and Dr. Mansi, who visit daily.

The Durbar Hall:
Situated in the center of the main building, the Durbar Hall houses portraits of some of the original donors and General Sir Henry Daly. This majestic facility is where dignitaries are welcomed, and important meetings and events are held.

All of them encountered and absorbed the fundamental elements and principles of art—embracing concepts such as balance, contrast, rhythm, proportion, unity, and more. These principles extend beyond the canvas, offering valuable insights that can be applied to the broader principles of life.

In IPSC, Visual Art competition hosted by Scindia Kanya Vidyalaya, Gwalior, Saara Lunawat and Riddhima Jain earned the prestigious Award for Artistic Excellence in Graffiti Art and Pranav Reji and Hartejas Sarup secured the award for Artistic Excellence in Driftwood Art. Rishaan Bhatia also won the Inspiring Artistry Award in Human Flower Photography.
Adding to the commendable achievements, in Srijanyam Art and Design Fest hosted by Assam Valley School, securing the 1st position, Preksha Modi and Aadhyaa Gurnani excelled in artificial flower making, while Chaheti Rathore and Aadityansh Mittal claimed the top spot in Sculpture. In the domain of product design, Hetal Shastri, Aadhyaa Gurnani, and Preksha Modi demonstrated their prowess, earning them the 1st position. Meanwhile, Kaashvi Dhawan achieved the 2nd position in cultural photojournalism. Notably, Aradhya Agrawal, Krishna Gupta, and Trisha Khurana each secured the 3rd position in Portrait painting, Caricature, and Mathematical Art, respectively. Daly College clinched the title of Overall Champion at the Srijanyam Art and Design Fest for the first time.
Furthermore, the Dalians also grabbed the opportunity to learn the famous Bell Metal or Dhokra Art of the Bastar region under the guidance of the master craftsman, Sri Bhupendra Baghel fostering an appreciation of craftsmanship, cultural heritage, and the significance of preserving traditional arts.
Inspired by Albert Einstein’s insight of creativity being contagious, the Dalians are actively embracing and planning to infuse a passion for art across the campus. This initiative has already taken shape with the installation of the metal sculpture, ‘The Clouds’ which is a symbolic representation of aspiration, ambition, achievement, and effort. The whole structure reminds one of a human being, yet there is a hand in place of the human head. This ambiguity is open to imagination and personal interpretations. Echoing the sentiment expressed by Gilbert K. Chesterton, "There are no rules of architecture for a castle in the clouds," this artistic endeavour embodies boundless creativity and invites a realm of limitless possibilities. Initiated by Dr. Gunmeet Bindra, Principal, Daly College, this sculpture is conceived and created by Mr. Rishi Barua, ex-faculty, Kala Bhavana, Visva-Bharati University, Santiniketan and Daly College students. It was inaugurated by Maharaj Vikram Singhji Puar of Dewas Senior and the veteran actor, Mr Kabir Bedi.
The remarkable efforts of Mrs. Arpita Dasgupta and Mrs. Priyanka Dave shaped this enriching journey, nurturing a lasting passion and profound affection for the realm of art within each individual.

ROUND SQUARE INTERNATIONAL SERVICE PROJECT IN THAILAND:
One of our students, Shivang Doval attended a Round Square International Service Project in Thailand which was scheduled from December 5 – 10, 2023. The RSIS Thailand 2023 project was attended by 59 students from 14 countries spread across the five Round Square regions.
This Round Square International Service Project included the construction of a community research centre, park ranger huts, look-out towers, hornbill nest boxes and beehive fences. The team also helped to plant lemongrass and citrus plants, plus other crops hated by snacky elephants.
Students learned about the Thai culture and history dating back as far as the Mangrai dynasty and how small changes can have a big impact in rural communities. In addition to learning about elephant behaviour, demographics, and migration routes, they also learned about the importance of local fauna and flora.
After the project they walked to the Chao Phraya River pier for a Bangkok Klong tour on a long tail boat. They visited Wat Po, home to the golden reclining Buddha and traditional Thai massage.
